# Temporal composition

Treat animated elements, layers, repeated surfaces, or complete Patterns as voices playing one score.

## Nested time scales

- **Hit:** brief punctuation or the smallest visible event.
- **Pulse:** recurring motion that establishes tempo.
- **Development unit:** change density, direction, ownership, scale, or masking.
- **Phrase or section:** change the governing system.
- **Arc:** complete tension, rupture, peak, and release.

Musical work may use literal beat and bar boundaries. A 32-bar, 128 BPM score lasts 60 seconds; eight four-bar phrases last 7.5 seconds each. This is a strong club-show default, not a universal requirement. Non-musical work still benefits from nested periodicity.

## Structural actions

- **Ignition:** reveal the grammar with minimum active material.
- **Lift:** add voices or density in discrete steps.
- **Countermotion:** establish opposition through direction, mirror, phase, or call-and-response.
- **Lock:** converge several voices on one hard law.
- **Vacuum:** remove density or expose a symbol; subtraction must feel authored.
- **Rebuild:** restore material through a canon or staircase rather than all at once.
- **Compression:** shorten cycles, tighten spacing, or reduce negative space.
- **Peak:** let the full system cohere, then punctuate and release.

## Stair-step discipline

Prefer a few readable states over continuous undifferentiated ramps: one voice, then two, then four; thin, medium, dense; separated, paired, locked. Let smaller cycles continue inside each state so the work remains alive.

For a single Pattern, distribute these actions across modes or a longer master cycle. For a Show, assign them to Scenes and transitions. In either case, define what changes at each boundary and what remains invariant.
